Excavation Risk Intelligence

Excavation risk, ticket by ticket

A live map that rates every planned excavation by how likely it is to hit an underground gas pipe, and how serious the impact would be if it did. This demonstration uses simulated data, not real customer information.

SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA - illustrative only. No real tickets, excavators, pipe, or utility. Geometry is public OpenStreetMap; all risk values are fabricated.

What you are looking at

  • A synthetic distribution network for Columbus, Ohio: mains following the street grid, service lines branching to buildings, drawn in neutral gray. Pipe lights up only inside a dig area, coloured by that pipe segment's own vulnerability - how likely a dig is to damage it, from its material, age, coating and diameter (green = newer or more robust, red = most vulnerable); how much its loss would matter is carried by the ticket colour and popup. Click any pipe for the breakdown.
  • One synthetic day of excavation tickets, each flagged High / Moderate / Low priority.
  • Click any ticket to see the two headline indicators and the eight driver pills - excavator history, pipe vulnerability, work activity, locate quality, service disruption, critical facilities, business district, and transmission - each with a plain-language reason.

How the demo data is made

Street and building geometry is real, public OpenStreetMap data. Everything else - pipe material, vintage, diameter, vulnerability, tickets, excavators, and risk scores - is generated from published industry references (PHMSA distribution-system statistics, the Common Ground Alliance DIRT damage reports) and clearly-labelled engineering defaults. No proprietary utility data is used anywhere.
